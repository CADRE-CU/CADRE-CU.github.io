Higher Education

Quasi-experimental and policy research on financial aid, FAFSA, college enrollment, and completion — with a particular focus on the gaps that separate historically underserved students from their more privileged peers.

Overview

Going to college, paying for it, and finishing remain three of the most consequential decisions a young person will make — and three of the most unequal.

Our work in higher education uses administrative data, natural experiments, and randomized trials to ask how policy choices and program design shape these decisions. We pay close attention to the populations whose outcomes are most sensitive to policy change: first-generation students, low-income students, and students of color whose pathways through postsecondary education have historically been the most precarious.

The questions are economic in form but educational in stakes: which financial aid policies actually move enrollment? When students leave college, what does it cost them to come back? How do FAFSA changes ripple through the application pipeline? And how do families make sense of the financial bet that college now represents?

Questions guiding our work

  • How do financial aid programs — federal, state, and institutional — shape college enrollment and completion for underserved students?
  • What happens when students withdraw from college, and how do current Title IV rules affect their ability to return?
  • How is the Better FAFSA reshaping who applies, where they apply, and which colleges they enroll in?
  • What are the longer-run financial consequences of college for students and the families that support them?
Research strands

Where we focus inside higher education.

Four overlapping areas of inquiry that anchor current and recent work.

$

Financial Aid Policy

Federal and state aid programs — Pell, Title IV, promise programs, and need-based scholarships — and how their design choices affect enrollment, persistence, and completion.

↗

College Access & Enrollment

How students from historically underserved backgrounds find their way into postsecondary education, and where information, cost, and process barriers shape that pathway.

◎

Persistence & Completion

What keeps students enrolled, what causes them to leave, and how the rules governing withdrawal and re-entry shape their long-run educational attainment.

⌂

Family & Household Economics

How the costs and benefits of college are absorbed across student and parent finances, and what that means for understanding college as a household investment.

Lead faculty

Who's leading this work at CADRE.

OG

Oded Gurantz

Faculty, Research & Evaluation Methodology · School of Education

Oded Gurantz is an economist and education policy researcher whose work investigates the gaps in college enrollment and completion that separate students from historically underserved groups from their more privileged peers. He uses quasi-experimental and experimental research designs to estimate the causal effects of financial aid programs and admissions policies on student outcomes.

His current work spans Title IV withdrawal rules, the implementation of the Better FAFSA, and the financial effects of college on students and their families. He works in long-term partnerships with state agencies, federal partners, and nonprofit organizations to ensure that findings translate into policy change.

Methods & approach

How we answer these questions.

Our work draws on the full toolkit of applied economics and education policy research, combined with the practitioner partnerships that make causal questions tractable.

Quasi-experimental designs Regression discontinuity, difference-in-differences, instrumental variables
Randomized trials Field experiments embedded in policy and program rollouts
Administrative data Linked records from federal, state, and institutional partners
Policy-engaged research Long-term partnerships with agencies that translate findings into practice
