Classroom Assessment

How teachers and students use assessment as part of teaching and learning — to make student thinking visible, to guide instruction in the moment, and to capture what students can actually do.

Overview

The most consequential assessments students experience are not the ones that show up on accountability reports — they are the ones that happen inside classrooms, every day, as part of instruction.

Our work in classroom assessment treats assessment as an integral part of teaching: a way to surface student thinking, to design tasks that capture meaningful performances, and to give teachers and students the information they need to act. We draw on cognitive and sociocultural theories of learning, on partnerships with teachers and districts, and on a long tradition of measurement scholarship at CU Boulder that has shaped how the field thinks about assessment in service of learning.

Across our projects, we ask not just whether assessments are technically defensible, but whether they help students learn — and whether they treat all students fairly in the process.

Questions guiding our work

  • How can teachers use formative assessment to make student thinking visible and act on it in the moment?
  • What does it look like to design performance and authentic assessments that capture what students can really do?
  • How can learning progressions connect curriculum, instruction, and the assessments that follow from them?
  • How do we ensure classroom assessment surfaces — rather than masks — equity issues for emergent bilingual learners and historically underserved students?
Research strands

Where we focus inside classroom assessment.

Four overlapping areas of inquiry that anchor current and recent work.

↻

Formative Assessment in Practice

How teachers elicit student thinking, interpret it, and respond to it — and how schools build systems and routines that make this kind of teaching sustainable.

★

Performance & Authentic Assessment

Designing tasks — capstones, performance assessments, project-based work — that ask students to demonstrate what they can actually do, and the systems that support their use.

↗

Learning Progressions

How student understanding develops over time, and how progressions can anchor curriculum, instruction, and assessment in a shared model of growth.

⚖

Classroom Assessment for Equity

Building assessments that work for emergent bilingual learners and historically underserved students — and that connect classroom evidence to the decisions made about students' futures.

Lead faculty

Who's leading this work at CADRE.

This focal area draws on a long tradition of classroom assessment scholarship at CU Boulder, spanning measurement theory, science education, and applied work with state and district partners.

LS

Lorrie A. Shepard

Distinguished Professor Emerita & Dean Emerita · Research & Evaluation Methodology

A foundational figure in the field, Shepard's work argues that classroom assessment should be an integral part of instruction — drawing on cognitive and sociocultural learning theory to reframe assessment as a tool for surfacing and addressing student misconceptions. Past president of AERA and the National Academy of Education.

Profile Selected work
EF

Erin Marie Furtak

Professor & Faculty Chair · Associate Dean of Faculty

Furtak studies how science teachers learn to design and enact formative assessment to support more inclusive participation, with particular attention to emergent bilingual learners. She leads the NSF/Spencer-funded Aspire Partnership and the NSF CAREER-funded Elevate Project on learning progressions in natural selection.

Profile Selected work
DB

Derek C. Briggs

Director, CADRE · Professor, Research & Evaluation Methodology

Briggs's work on classroom assessment centers on validity, learning progressions, and how the connections among curriculum, instruction, and assessment can be made explicit and tested. His research on developmental models of cognition and content-referenced growth is shaping how the field interprets what students have learned.

Profile Selected work
ED

Elena Diaz-Bilello

Associate Director, CADRE · Assistant Research Professor

Diaz-Bilello partners with state agencies and districts to design and evaluate performance and authentic assessment systems — from Colorado's graduation-readiness work to performance assessments developed by classroom teachers in Hawai'i. Her background bridges district, state, and national assessment policy.

Methods & approach

How we answer these questions.

Our work draws on classroom-based research methods that take seriously what teachers and students actually do — combined with the partnerships that make sustained study possible.

Design-based research Iterative co-design of assessments and instructional materials with teachers
Research-practice partnerships Long-term collaborations with schools, districts, and state agencies
Mixed-methods classroom studies Video, observation, sequential analysis of discourse, and student work
Validity & validation Building evidence for what assessments support what claims about learning
