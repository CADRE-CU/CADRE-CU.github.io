Classroom Assessment

Assessment that is part of teaching. Classroom assessment is the work teachers do to find out what students understand during a lesson, and to decide what to teach next. CADRE designs and studies classroom assessment to support this work, in partnership with teachers, schools, districts, and state agencies.

What we think good classroom assessment requires

Equitable assessment practices are ambitious teaching practices.

In 2020 CADRE published Classroom Assessment Principles to Support Teaching and Learning, by Lorrie Shepard, Elena Diaz-Bilello, William Penuel, and Scott Marion. The authors drafted the principles for the NCME Special Conference on Classroom Assessment, which CADRE hosted at CU Boulder in September 2019, and revised them afterward using participants' input.

The principles rest on sociocultural learning theory, which explains why context, culture, and a sense of belonging are part of how students learn. Asset-based, culturally responsive, and culturally sustaining teaching all sit inside that frame. The principles cover formative practice during a unit and the summative assessment used for grades and competency decisions. They are written for school leaders and for district and state policymakers.

When the principles were first circulated, one criticism was that they looked mostly like good instruction. That was the intention. The principles continue to guide our research, our partnerships, and the technical assistance we provide.

Projects

Past and current classroom assessment work.

With schools, districts, states, and national organizations.

Active · Hawaiʻi DOE · Center for Assessment

Designing performance assessment with Hawaiʻi ELA teachers

English language arts teachers across Hawaiʻi are building classroom performance assessments with us. The assessments are designed to support Nā Hopena Aʻo, a framework of learner outcomes grounded in Hawaiian values, language, culture, and history, adopted by the Hawaiʻi Board of Education in 2015. A collaboration with the Hawaiʻi Department of Education and the National Center for the Improvement of Educational Assessment.

Visit the task library
Active · Pueblo School District 60

Scaling project-based learning in Pueblo School District 60

Pueblo School District 60 has instituted project-based learning in several schools with the intent to continue scaling. We work with the district on continuous improvement: testing changes, checking which changes hold up in different schools, and building the district's capacity to carry the work forward.

Active · Colorado Department of Education

Studying the enactment of high-quality instructional materials

Districts spend heavily on high-quality instructional materials. We study what happens when those materials reach classrooms: how teachers adapt them, what support teachers need, and how teachers use the assessments that come with the materials.

Completed · Class CrunchLabs

Feasibility and usability of new Class CrunchLabs science units

Class CrunchLabs has produced new science units for grades 6 to 8. We studied whether teachers can teach the units as written, what gets in the way, and whether the assessments inside the units help teachers see student thinking.

Completed · NCME · CU Boulder, September 2019

NCME Special Conference on Classroom Assessment

CADRE hosted the conference at CU Boulder on September 18 and 19, 2019. Measurement researchers met with staff from Cherry Creek School District, Aurora Public Schools, Denver Public Schools, and the Colorado Department of Education. The classroom assessment principles described above were drafted for this conference and revised afterward using what participants said.

What's new

Recent and upcoming.

September 29, 2026 · NCME SIG talk

How should classroom assessment be defined to engage deep and meaningful learning?

Lorrie Shepard will present to the NCME special interest group on Assessment and Measurement to Support Classroom Learning, with support from Elena Diaz-Bilello. The talk asks how the field should define classroom assessment if the goal is deep learning, and why that definition shapes the assessment products that reach classrooms.

Forthcoming · Handbook chapter

Expanding what counts: Culturally relevant and sustaining classroom assessment

Erin Furtak and Elena Diaz-Bilello have a chapter coming out in the second edition of the Classroom Assessment Handbook; the first edition appeared in 2013. The chapter takes a systems and ecological view of classroom assessment as a relational practice embedded in instruction, and considers what it means to align that practice with culturally relevant and sustaining frameworks.
