Educational Accountability

Growth models, school performance frameworks, and educator evaluation — and the design choices that determine whether these systems fairly reflect what schools and teachers contribute to student learning.

Overview

Accountability systems turn test scores into judgments about schools and educators. Whether those judgments are fair depends on technical decisions most people never see.

CADRE has worked on the measurement side of accountability for two decades: how student growth is modeled, how vertical scales and value-added models interact, how indicators are weighted into composite ratings, and what happens to small schools and student groups when minimum-count rules are applied. Much of this work is for the Colorado Department of Education, where CADRE provides ongoing technical assistance to the state's accountability unit.

We approach accountability as a validity problem. The question is never only whether a statistic is computed correctly, but whether the inference it supports about a school, a teacher, or a program can survive scrutiny, and what the system's incentives do to the behavior it is meant to measure.

Questions guiding our work

  • How should growth be modeled so that it isolates what schools contribute rather than whom they enroll?
  • When indicators are combined into a composite rating, do the weights that are stated match the weights that actually drive the result?
  • What are the tradeoffs between reporting results for small student groups and the reliability of what gets reported?
  • How do educator evaluation systems that combine growth measures, observations, and professional judgment behave in practice?
Research strands

Where we focus inside accountability.

Four overlapping areas of inquiry that anchor current and recent work.

↗

Growth Models

Student growth percentiles, vertical-scale gain scores, and value-added models: what each measures, how sensitive each is to design choices, and how results should be reported.

▦

School Performance Frameworks

The construction of composite school ratings, nominal versus effective indicator weights, minimum-count thresholds, and the stability of ratings from year to year.

✓

Educator Evaluation

Evidence from Denver's ProComp, Colorado's student learning objectives pilot, and studies of how principal judgments compare with growth and observation measures.

⚖

Policy & Consequences

Campbell's law, the incentives accountability creates, and the design of federal and state testing policy that supports improvement rather than distortion.

Lead faculty & staff

Who's leading this work at CADRE.

This area joins CADRE's psychometric expertise with long-standing relationships with the Colorado Department of Education and other state agencies.

DB

Derek C. Briggs

Director, CADRE · Professor, Research & Evaluation Methodology

Briggs has studied growth and value-added modeling since the mid-2000s, including the sensitivity of value-added estimates to vertical scale design, the persistence of school effects, and the use of student growth percentiles for educator evaluation. He has advised state agencies in Hawai'i and Colorado and reviewed high-profile uses of value-added rankings for the National Education Policy Center.

Profile Measurement work
BS

Benjamin R. Shear

Associate Professor, Research & Evaluation Methodology · CADRE Faculty Partner

Shear leads CADRE's technical assistance to the Colorado Department of Education's accountability unit. His recent working papers examine nominal and effective weights in Colorado's School Performance Framework, the relationship between minimum student counts and the reliability of school-level measures, and methods for stabilizing school test score metrics.

Profile Personal site
ED

Elena Diaz-Bilello

Associate Director, CADRE · Assistant Research Professor

Before joining CADRE, Diaz-Bilello was a Senior Associate at the National Center for the Improvement of Educational Assessment and a consultant to the Colorado Department of Education on accountability and educator effectiveness. She co-authored Colorado's guidance on using student growth percentiles in teacher evaluation and now bridges CADRE's accountability and evaluation work.

Kaitlin Nath

Methods & approach

How we answer these questions.

Our work pairs the statistical machinery of growth and composite modeling with sustained, embedded technical assistance to the agencies that run these systems.

Growth & value-added modeling Student growth percentiles, vertical-scale gains, and multilevel value-added models
Reliability & stability analysis Minimum-count thresholds, small-sample reliability, and year-to-year volatility of ratings
Composite index design Nominal versus effective weights, indicator correlation, and rating construction
Policy analysis & technical assistance Embedded support for state agencies, review of high-stakes uses, and public comment

Designing or revising an accountability system?

CADRE provides technical assistance to state and district agencies on growth models, performance frameworks, and educator evaluation, and reviews proposed systems before they take effect.
