Technical Advice

Independent review for the assessment programs that states and the nation rely on. CADRE faculty and staff serve on technical advisory committees for thirteen states, two assessment consortia, two NAEP panels, and two assessment developers.

Overview

Every large-scale testing program depends on a small group of outside experts to ask hard questions before scores reach the public. That is what a technical advisory committee does, and CADRE staff sit on many of them.

A technical advisory committee, or TAC, is a standing group of measurement specialists who review a testing program's design, psychometrics, and score uses: how tests are built and scaled, how scores are equated from one year to the next, how performance standards are set, and whether the accountability decisions that ride on the results can be defended. Members serve as critical friends, independent of both the vendor and the agency, and obligated to say what the evidence does and does not support.

Derek Briggs, Elena Diaz-Bilello, and Ben Shear currently serve on committees for thirteen states, the Smarter Balanced and Multistate Alternate Assessment consortia, two NAEP panels, and two assessment developers. Briggs has also written about how TACs work and what they have achieved, from the Race to the Top assessment consortia to the public list of state TAC members that NCME released in 2025.

What the work involves

Four kinds of committee.

▦

State Assessment Programs

Standing committees that review test design, scaling and equating, standard setting, score reporting, and the accountability uses that depend on the results.

◎

Consortia & National Assessment

The Smarter Balanced and Multistate Alternate Assessment consortia, and the two NAEP panels that examine the design and validity of the Nation's Report Card.

↗

Assessment Developers

Advisory committees for Curriculum Associates and Edmentum, where research on growth reporting and interim assessment meets commercial practice.

§

Scholarship on Advising

Writing that documents what TACs do, how the Race to the Top assessment consortia fared, and how the field can make technical advice more transparent.

Who serves

CADRE's technical advisors.

Three perspectives on the same table: a psychometrician, a former state and district assessment leader, and a specialist in accountability system design.

DB

Derek C. Briggs

Director, CADRE · Professor, Research & Evaluation Methodology

Briggs has served on state technical advisory committees since 2006 and was an inaugural member of both the PARCC and Smarter Balanced TACs in 2011. He chairs the Multistate Alternate Assessment Consortium's TAC and sits on NAEP's Design Analysis Committee and Validity Studies Panel. His 2024 history of the assessment consortia in Educational Measurement: Issues and Practice draws on that service, and he is a co-author of TACtics, an article under review on how state TACs work.

Profile Measurement work
ED

Elena Diaz-Bilello

Associate Director, CADRE · Assistant Research Professor

Diaz-Bilello brings a state-agency and district perspective to committee service, drawing on her years as a Senior Associate at the National Center for the Improvement of Educational Assessment, a program evaluator for Denver Public Schools, and a consultant to the Colorado Department of Education. She currently advises six states: Alabama, Alaska, Kentucky, Louisiana, Nevada, and New Jersey.

Profile Evaluation work
BS

Benjamin R. Shear

Associate Professor, Research & Evaluation Methodology · CADRE Faculty Partner

Shear advises Arizona, Colorado, Illinois, and Montana, and serves on Edmentum's technical advisory committee. His committee work draws on his research on accountability system design, including CADRE's technical assistance to Colorado's accountability unit and working papers on composite ratings and the reliability of small-sample school measures.

What a committee gets from us

How we approach the work.

Independent psychometric review Design, scaling, equating, and standard-setting decisions examined before they take effect
Validity argumentation Evidence weighed against each intended score use, from student reports to school ratings
Plain-language translation Technical findings written so boards, legislators, and the press can act on them
Institutional memory Continuity across vendor transitions and policy changes, informed by service since 2006

Convening or refreshing a technical advisory committee?

CADRE faculty serve as independent members and chairs of TACs for state, consortium, and national assessment programs, and can help an agency structure a committee's charge.
